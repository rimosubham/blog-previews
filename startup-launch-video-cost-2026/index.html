How Much Does a Startup Launch Video Cost in 2026?

Real, published prices from launch video studios and agencies, what each tier actually buys, what getting the video seen costs on top, and how to set a budget for your stage.

Subah Wadhwani · Co-founder & CEO at Atomik Growth, the company behind The Launch Video CompanyUpdated 8 Oct 2026 · 17 min read · Checked against 17 sources
Featured image: startup launch video cost by tier, 2026 (placeholder)

In short: Based on prices agencies publish, a startup launch video costs about $875–$5,000 for a simple or animated video, $5,000–$12,500 for a studio-made launch video with no distribution, $15,000–$25,000 for a filmed launch video that includes launch-day distribution with a guaranteed view floor, and $25,000+ for full-crew productions. Distribution bought on its own runs from about $150 for a single creator repost to a typical $30–$50 per thousand views for managed X amplification. Multi-million-view guarantees, including The Launch Video Company's 1M, 3M and 5M tiers, are quoted on a call. Compare quotes on cost per outcome, not cost per minute.

Disclosure: this guide is published by The Launch Video Company, which sells launch videos and distribution. We don't publish our own prices, so we haven't invented a number for ourselves. Every other price below comes from the agency's own website as of 8 October 2026. Prices change, so check before you buy.

DIY and AI tools: $0–$500

A founder-shot screen recording with captions costs an afternoon. AI video tools can turn a script into a draft in minutes. This is the right choice for pre-product teams, internal demos and quick tests. It rarely works for a big launch moment, because a generic-looking video struggles to earn the first three seconds in a feed. It's still worth doing first: a rough cut tells you whether the story works before you pay anyone.

Entry studio: $875–$5,000

At this level you're buying a finished file with limited strategy. ReactivVibeAI's published price list starts at $875 for one product film up to three minutes, delivered in 5–7 business days with one revision round. Dammann.ai's Product Film is $2,700 with no view guarantee. What a Story says its premium SaaS projects start at $3,500. Expect few revisions, and expect to write much of the brief yourself.

Launch studio, production only: $5,000–$12,500

This is the sweet spot for a polished launch video when you plan to run distribution yourself.

  • Flowjam publishes a $5,000 animated 60-second launch video delivered in 10 days, with script, voiceover, custom music and unlimited revisions included (pricing).
  • Unveil Studio publishes three packages: Launch Pro at $5k (you shoot it, or it's fully animated), Cinematic Launch at $8k (they film you in SF or NY), and Narrative Launch at $12k, with delivery in 5–9 business days and a refund if you're unhappy at the styleframe stage.
  • LaunchAnything sells one launch video of up to 90 seconds for $12,500, including help with the launch post copy. Its site says publishing and distribution stay with you.

Video plus guaranteed distribution: $5,700–$25,000

Some studios now bundle a launch-day push with a written view floor.

  • Dammann.ai lists a Launch Film at $5,700 (30,000+ views promised, 7 days), Launch Film Pro at $14,700 (150,000+, 14 days) and Launch Film Max at $22,700 (400,000+, 14 days).
  • Flowjam lists $15,000 for an on-camera launch film plus distribution with a 100,000-view minimum, and $25,000 for the same film with a 500,000-view minimum. Filmed plans need at least 14 days.

These are useful benchmarks for what a guaranteed view costs. At list price, Flowjam's two tiers work out to about $0.15 and $0.05 per guaranteed view, and Dammann.ai's tiers to roughly $0.19, $0.10 and $0.06. Those figures include the video itself, so they aren't pure distribution costs.

Full-crew production: $15,000–$45,000+

Bigger budgets buy more shoot days, crew, locations and versions. Clickstrike's Launch Video Studio starts at $25,000 and runs 4–6 weeks from kickoff, filming your team on location, with X amplification and PR as optional extras. WERZ says its launch and investor films typically run $15,000–$45,000, with delivery in 1–6 weeks. ReactivVibeAI's Full Launch (a site, four films and 12 cutdowns) is $24,000.

Quote-based, outcome-led launches

At the top of the distribution market, agencies sell an outcome rather than a file, and quote it after a call.

  • The Launch Video Company (us) sells guaranteed 1M, 3M or 5M view launches on X and LinkedIn, either with full production or as amplification only. Pricing isn't published; it's quoted on an intro call based on scope and budget.
  • FORKOFF guarantees 1.2M, 3M or 5M views with a make-good, prices on the outcome (views, then pipeline) and takes clients by application.

If you need a hard number before a call, the published guaranteed-view packages above are the closest public benchmarks. Just remember that a 100,000-view floor and a 3M-view guarantee are different products, so they won't cost the same.

The two halves of a launch video budget: production and distribution

Most cost guides only price the file. But a launch video has two bills: making it and getting it seen. FORKOFF's guide to what a launch video actually costs makes the same point: production is widely quoted, distribution rarely is. Clickstrike's product launch video cost guide puts it bluntly: "A $10,000 video with $15,000 of amplification behind it will beat a $25,000 video with nothing behind it in almost every launch we have run."

That matches what we see. A beautiful video posted from a company account with a few hundred followers usually reaches a few thousand people. On X and LinkedIn, the first hour decides how far a post travels, so the people who engage in that hour matter more than the production budget.

A quick way to sanity-check any amplification quote: divide the price by the guaranteed views (in thousands) to get an effective CPM, then ask how a "view" is counted and over what time window. Debuts, for example, counts impressions reported by X in the 14 days after each creator's post. Clickstrike reports delivered CPM after the campaign.

If creators are paid to post, they should disclose it. The FTC's Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers says a disclosure belongs with the post itself and should be hard to miss. Ask any distribution partner how its network handles this.

What drives the cost of a launch video?

Two quotes for "a 60-second launch video" can differ by 10x. Usually that's because they describe different jobs. These are the line items that move the number.

Vidico's product video cost guide is a useful wider benchmark. It puts a product launch commercial at $5,000–$15,000 at the budget end (single location, small crew, one or two shoot days, which it says suits startups launching a first product), $15,000–$50,000 mid-range, and $50,000–$200,000+ at the premium end.

Hidden costs to check before you sign

  • Revision overages. Ask what an extra round costs and what counts as a "round".
  • Rush fees. If your date is fixed and close, ask for the all-in price with a fast delivery.
  • Licensing. Music, stock footage and fonts should be licensed for paid use if you'll run ads.
  • Cutdowns. Vertical, square, 6–15 second versions and captions are often extra.
  • Raw files and rights. Ask for footage, project files and a written copyright assignment. Under U.S. law a commissioned video only counts as a "work made for hire" in certain categories and with a signed agreement, so put ownership in the contract.
  • Your own time. Script reviews, shoot day and launch-day replies cost founder hours. A cheap vendor you have to project-manage isn't always cheaper.

How much should a startup spend on a launch video, by stage?

There's no single right number, but here's a practical way to think about it. These are our recommendations, based on the published prices above and the launches we've run.

Stage Sensible launch budget Where the money should go
Pre-seed / bootstrapped $0–$5,000 A founder-shot or entry-studio video. Spend effort on your own network, communities and Product Hunt rather than polish
Seed $5,000–$25,000 A studio launch video, plus some form of launch-day distribution, either bundled or bought separately
Series A+ $25,000+ Full production with a guaranteed distribution plan, cutdowns for paid, and re-cuts for later launches
Fundraise or Demo Day announcement Match to the outcome you need If one day decides investor or customer pipeline, weight the budget towards distribution

Other publishers land in a similar place. FORKOFF estimates $0–$3,000 as a sensible total at pre-seed. Flowjam's cost guide suggests $1,000–$5,000 for most seed and Series A launch videos, and reserves $40,000+ for flagship brand films. Treat all of these, including ours, as rules of thumb.

YC's Kat Manalac makes a related point in How to launch (again and again): launching isn't a single moment, and most startups will launch something that "no one will care" about at first. She also warns early founders off pay-to-play influencer deals. So if you're very early, launch often and cheaply. Save the big budget for the launch that really matters.

How to budget so the video actually gets seen

Here are three ways to split a budget. The scenarios are illustrations, not quotes.

Scenario 1: $5,000 and a small audience. Spend about $2,000–$3,000 on a tight, founder-led or entry-studio video and keep $2,000–$3,000 for distribution: a handful of niche creator reposts, plus a planned first hour with your investors, advisors and customers. Write the post copy as carefully as the video.

Scenario 2: $15,000–$25,000, a raise to announce. Buy a bundled package with a written view floor, or a production-only video plus a separately priced amplification plan. Compare the two on effective cost per guaranteed view, and on whether the engaging accounts match your buyers and investors.

Scenario 3: you already have the video. Don't pay to make it again. Buy amplification only. At a $30–$50 CPM, 500,000 views would cost about $15,000–$25,000 at Clickstrike's published typical rate. Ask any provider (including us) for an equivalent plan and compare like for like.

Then measure the result as cost per outcome: total spend (production plus distribution) divided by the number you care about. That might be qualified views, waitlist signups, demo requests or pre-orders. A $20,000 launch that produces 500 demo requests costs $40 per demo. A $5,000 video that produces 20 costs $250 per demo. The cheaper video was the more expensive launch.

Some reference points from our own launches (self-reported, as every agency's are): the Enrich Labs launch reached 3.6M+ people with 10× homepage traffic and 9,000+ signups and customers. The HydraDB launch reached 2M views in under two hours (3.7M total) with hundreds of demo requests. The Mave Health launch, for a $500 headset, drove six figures of pre-orders. We don't publish what those clients paid.

How to get launch video quotes you can actually compare

Send every vendor the same one-page brief:

  1. The launch: what you're announcing, the date, and whether the date can move.
  2. The goal: one number, for example "1,000 waitlist signups in 48 hours" or "50 demo requests".
  3. The format: founder-led, animated, or a mix; target length (most launch videos run 30–90 seconds).
  4. Deliverables: hero cut, vertical and square versions, 6–15 second cutdowns, captions, thumbnail.
  5. Distribution: do you want them to run it, and on which platforms? What's guaranteed, how are views counted, and what happens if the number is missed?
  6. Rights: final files, raw footage, project files and copyright assignment.
  7. Revisions and rush: the all-in price with three rounds of changes and your real delivery date.

Then line the quotes up in one table, with production and distribution as separate rows. You'll quickly see which quotes include what.

Red flags in a launch video quote

  • A view guarantee without a definition of a "view", a time window, or make-good terms.
  • Showreels instead of links to live launch posts you can check.
  • Engagement that doesn't match reach: millions of views with very few replies or quote posts deserve a question.
  • "Unlimited" anything with no written scope.
  • A timeline quoted in months for a launch that's weeks away.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a startup launch video cost in 2026?+

Published prices run from $875 for a simple product film to $5,000–$12,500 for a studio launch video without distribution, $15,000–$25,000 for a filmed launch with a guaranteed view floor, and $25,000+ for full-crew productions. Multi-million-view guarantees are quoted on a call.

What's the cheapest way to make a startup launch video?+

A founder-shot video or an AI-tool draft costs $0–$500. The cheapest published studio option we found is $875 for a product film up to three minutes. Cheap works for small updates; for a big launch, put any savings into distribution.

How much does a launch video with distribution cost?+

Published bundles range from $5,700 (30,000+ views promised) to $25,000 (500,000-view minimum). Flowjam charges $15,000 and $25,000 for 100,000 and 500,000 views. The Launch Video Company and FORKOFF quote multi-million-view tiers on a call.

How much does X amplification cost on its own?+

Clickstrike publishes a typical CPM of $30–$50, or about $30–$50 per 1,000 views, with a make-good clause. Single creator reposts start around $150 each at Debuts. The Launch Video Company offers amplification only on a quote basis.

Is an animated launch video cheaper than a filmed one?+

Usually, yes. Filming adds crew, location, equipment and travel costs. Flowjam estimates filming adds $5,000–$15,000 of overhead, and its animated launch video costs $5,000 against $15,000 for its filmed plan with distribution.

Why do launch video quotes vary so much?+

Quotes for the same length can describe different jobs: strategy or no strategy, animation or filming, one version or twenty, and with or without distribution. Line up quotes by deliverables and guarantees, not by runtime.

How much should a seed-stage startup spend on a launch video?+

As a rule of thumb, $5,000–$25,000 all-in for a launch that matters, with part of it set aside for distribution. Pre-seed teams can launch well for $0–$5,000 by leaning on their own networks and communities.

Does the price of a launch video include distribution?+

Usually not. Most studios deliver the file only. Flowjam's $15k and $25k plans, Dammann.ai's Launch Film tiers, and agencies like The Launch Video Company and FORKOFF include distribution. Always ask.

What hidden costs should I expect?+

Extra revision rounds ($250–$1,000 each at some vendors), rush fees (25–50% with some traditional vendors), music and stock licensing, extra cutdowns, and access to raw files. Ask for the all-in price with three rounds of changes and your real delivery date.

How long does a startup launch video take to make?+

Published timelines run from 5–7 business days (ReactivVibeAI's product film) and 5–9 business days (Unveil) to 4–6 weeks (Clickstrike). End-to-end production with distribution usually takes 2–3 weeks.

Is it cheaper to hire an in-house video editor?+

Not for one launch. The U.S. median wage for film and video editors was $75,420 in May 2025, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, before equipment and software. In-house makes sense once you produce video every week.

How do I compare a production-only quote with a bundled one?+

Split each quote into production and distribution, then divide the total by the guaranteed or expected outcome, such as views, signups or demos. Also check how views are counted and what happens if the guarantee is missed.

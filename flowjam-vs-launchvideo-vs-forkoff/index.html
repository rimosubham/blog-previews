Flowjam vs LaunchVideo vs FORKOFF (2026): An Honest Comparison

Three launch video agencies that all promise views on X, compared on price, guarantees, timelines, proof and the fine print.

Subah Wadhwani · Co-founder & CEO at Atomik Growth, the company behind The Launch Video CompanyUpdated 8 Oct 2026 · 19 min read · Checked against 18 sources
Featured image: Flowjam vs LaunchVideo vs FORKOFF comparison (placeholder)

In short: Flowjam, The Launch Video Company (LaunchVideo) and FORKOFF all make launch videos for startups and sell launch-day views on X. Flowjam is the price-transparent option: $5k for an animated video in 10 days, or $15k / $25k for a filmed launch with a 100,000 / 500,000-view minimum. LaunchVideo pairs founder-led production with curated X and LinkedIn amplification, guarantees 1M, 3M or 5M views, offers amplification-only, and quotes on a call. FORKOFF guarantees 1.2M, 3M or 5M views with a published make-good (re-run or refund), prices "on the outcome" by application, and wants about three weeks of runway. None of them has independently audited view counts. Pick on budget, runway, the view scale you need, and the contract terms.

Disclosure: this comparison is published by The Launch Video Company, which is one of the three agencies compared. We've tried to hold all three to the same standard, including where FORKOFF criticises us. Everything about Flowjam and FORKOFF comes from their own websites and third-party pages as they read on 8 October 2026. Where a company doesn't publish something, we say so rather than guess.

The short verdict: which one should you hire?

  • Choose Flowjam if you want to know the price before a call, you need an animated product video fast, and a 100,000 or 500,000-view floor is enough for your launch.
  • Choose The Launch Video Company if your launch has to reach millions of people on X or LinkedIn in the first day, you want the video and the amplification run by one team, or you already have a video and only need distribution.
  • Choose FORKOFF if a public, written make-good matters most to you, you have three or more weeks before launch, and you're comfortable with outcome-based pricing you only see after applying. Its client roster also leans towards crypto and web3, which may suit those launches.

The rest of this article shows the evidence behind each line, including the parts that don't flatter us.

How we compared them

We judged all three on the same seven things, using only what each company publishes or what a third party has written about them:

  1. What you actually buy: film only, film plus distribution, or distribution only.
  2. Price: published or not, and what it includes.
  3. The view guarantee: the number, how it's counted, and what happens if it's missed.
  4. Turnaround: the stated timeline and the fastest documented launch.
  5. Notable work: launches you can click through to and check.
  6. Proof: who measures the result, and whether anyone independent checks it.
  7. Fit: the kind of startup each one seems built for.

We didn't rank them by total views claimed. Every agency in this category reports its own numbers, and that includes us.

Flowjam: published prices and a modest view floor

What it is

Flowjam is a launch video studio run by founder and creative director Adam Petty, based in London and San Francisco. Its homepage says it makes "launch videos for AI startups" and that its videos have generated 39 million views across 100+ videos for 50+ YC startups. Adam writes the scripts himself and you deal with him throughout, with no account manager in between.

Pricing and what's included

Flowjam is the only one of the three that puts every price on its homepage:

Notable work

Flowjam's homepage credits HeyGen's Video Agent launch with 7M+ views in 48 hours, Bloom (YC) with 1.5M+ views in 24 hours, plus launches for Origami, Minerva, Anglera, BentoLabs, Kite and others. What the fine print says

  • Guarantee: Flowjam says the floors are "a floor, not a forecast" and that it puts them "in writing". Its site doesn't say how views are counted or what happens if a launch falls short. Ask before you sign.
  • Refunds: "we do not offer refunds". The answer to "I don't love it" is unlimited revisions.
  • Ownership: "You own it outright", with no licensing fees or usage windows.

Third-party view

An independent review by SIIT describes Flowjam as a specialist startup studio and notes its Product Hunt profile had a 4.5/5 rating from only two reviews, too few to judge by.

Pros: Clear prices before any call. Fast animated option. Founder-direct service. Ownership and revision terms in writing.

Cons: The view floors (100k / 500k) are well below the million-view tiers the other two sell. No published make-good. No refunds. No separate amplification-only option if you already have a video.

The Launch Video Company (LaunchVideo): production plus curated amplification

What it is

The Launch Video Company is a San Francisco launch video and distribution studio run by Atomik Growth, according to its llms.txt file. Its homepage describes the offer as "ideation, scripting, recording, editing, to guaranteeing millions of views on X & LinkedIn". The site lists four services: launch video production, organic distribution on X, organic distribution on LinkedIn, and amplification only for founders who made their own video.

How the distribution works

The homepage lays out four stages: creative strategy, scripting and production ("a concise, founder-led video built for X and LinkedIn. Not cinema for cinema's sake"), influencer preparation (a curated list of tech, founder, operator and investor accounts), and launch-day coordination of copy, timing and distribution.

Our own launch write-ups go further. In the Mave Health breakdown, creators were run in tiers, and the founder's own network came in after the first few hours. In the HydraDB write-up, influencers were checked for a 33%+ US audience, and paid posts used X's paid-post label "to stay within X's guidelines". That matters, and we come back to it below.

Pricing and guarantees

  • Price: not published. You book an intro call, and the site warns "We might have a waitlist."
  • Guarantee: "Guarantee 1M, 3M, or 5M views" on the homepage.
  • Make-good: the site doesn't publish what happens if a launch misses its tier. That's a fair gap to point out, and you should get the terms in writing before you sign, as you would with any agency.

Turnaround

Our launch video playbook puts end-to-end production with multiple variants at 2–3 weeks. Compressed launches have happened. HydraDB went from first call to launch in five days (3.7M views). Mave Health came to us on a Friday and launched the following Wednesday, as an amplification-only job.

Notable work

Every launch on the homepage links to its live post on X or LinkedIn. They include Howie Liu's Airtable "Founding 500" announcement (8.1M views), SubQ (12.7M), Sabi (5.7M), Superblocks 2.0 (4.5M), Enrich Labs' Helena (3.6M), Mave Health (2.5M) and Composio (2M). The Edison Scientific launch (6.9M) was amplification-only. The Enrich Labs case study reports 10× homepage traffic and 9,000+ sign-ups and customers. The Mave Health case study reports six figures of pre-orders. You can browse more past launches in the launch directory.

A July 2026 Chainwire release from Atomik Growth claims more than 130 million views across launch campaigns in the first 18 months, and 70,000+ signups within a week for SubQ. Chainwire is a press-release distribution service, so treat those figures as the company's own claims.

Pros: Production and distribution designed together by one team. Million-view scale on the guarantee. Amplification-only option. Proven compressed timelines. Every credited launch links to a live post you can inspect.

Cons: No public price. No published make-good terms. The view counts are self-reported, as with every agency here. Built for X and LinkedIn, not for TV spots or cinematic brand films.

FORKOFF: outcome pricing, a published make-good and its own audit tool

What it is

FORKOFF describes itself as "an AI agency for Web3 and AI ecosystems, based out of MENA and APAC", offering clipping, podcast distribution, founder-led content and launches. Its product launch video service page calls it "the product launch video agency that owns the distribution, not just the film". It offers the same four scopes as LaunchVideo: production, X distribution, LinkedIn distribution, and amplification only.

How the distribution works

FORKOFF's mechanism is built around a 14-day "cluster warm-up" of a named list of ideal-customer accounts from the founder's handle before launch. That's followed by "debate-principal tagging", "recap-account seeding" and live monitoring in the first 60–90 minutes. It says it uses "real accounts, never a pod".

Pricing and guarantees

  • Price: "outcome-priced", "by application", "capped slots, premium scope". FORKOFF says it publishes its pricing model (views, then pipeline) but not a number. You get that from a strategist.
  • Guarantee: 1.2M, 3M or 5M views by tier.
  • Make-good: stated publicly: "if a launch misses the tier we keep distributing and re-run the play until it lands, or we refund." Of the three, this is the clearest public statement of what happens on a miss.

Turnaround

Because the method depends on the 14-day warm-up, FORKOFF says founders "who arrive less than 21 days from launch with a cold cluster" are poorly placed for the guarantee. You get "a candid timeline, not a sales pitch". So plan on roughly three weeks.

Notable work

FORKOFF's press page lists crypto and web3 clients such as Berachain, Uniswap, Taiko and io.net. The launch service page shows "launch week" results for Linear, Polygon and Chainlink. These aren't linked to the underlying posts, and two of them don't add up on the page itself. Polygon is shown with "61" creator posts and then described as "81 coordinated creator posts". It shows 2.5K and 2.7K waitlist signups in the stat boxes for Polygon and Chainlink, but "1,900" in the bullets. We'd ask FORKOFF for the live post links before relying on them.

FORKOFF's headline scale figure, 5B+ views processed across its clipping network, comes from a July 2026 press release distributed through ACCESS Newswire. FORKOFF's own press page openly labels that release as a paid wire. An independent review on FindClout concluded FORKOFF is "a real operating business", but that its headline scale claims have "no third-party audit" published.

Pros: The clearest public make-good of the three. An amplification-only option. A detailed, written method. Openly states when your timeline is too short.

Cons: No price until you apply. Needs about three weeks of runway. Its own case figures aren't linked and are internally inconsistent. Its "audit" is run by FORKOFF itself (see below).

View guarantees compared: what each number really means

A view guarantee is only as useful as three details: how a view is counted, who reports it, and what happens if it's missed.

How X counts a view. According to X's own help page on view counts, anyone logged in who views a post counts as a view, wherever they see it and whether or not they follow you. Views "are not all unique": the same person on web and on mobile counts twice, and the author's own views count too. Promoted (paid) posts also show view counts. So a raw view number tells you how often a post was shown. It doesn't tell you how many distinct people saw it, or how many cared.

What X's analytics separate out. X's Post Activity Dashboard reports impressions, engagements and engagement rate for each post. Its CSV export splits organic and promoted metrics into separate rows. It also notes that numbers can take up to 36 hours to stabilise. Whichever agency you hire, ask for that export (or a screen-share of it) 48 hours after launch. That's the closest thing to an official receipt.

How the three compare on paper:

  • Flowjam's floors are the smallest (100k and 500k), but they're attached to a published price. That makes Flowjam's the only guarantee you can price per view before talking to anyone.
  • LaunchVideo's and FORKOFF's tiers are at similar scale (1M vs 1.2M, then 3M and 5M at both).
  • Only FORKOFF publishes what happens on a miss. If you're choosing between LaunchVideo and FORKOFF and the make-good is decisive, ask LaunchVideo to put its terms in writing and compare them side by side.

FORKOFF's criticism of screenshot-backed guarantees, and our response

FORKOFF's comparison page, FORKOFF vs The Launch Video Company, argues that we market "self-reported view counts" that aren't independently audited. It says our pricing is sales-gated, and that we have no Clutch, G2 or Trustpilot profile. Its wider pages say agencies like ours "prove reach with self-reported screenshots". Since we're publishing this comparison, we owe you a straight answer.

Where FORKOFF has a point:

  • Our view counts are self-reported. No independent auditor has checked them. The same is true of Flowjam's and FORKOFF's.
  • Our pricing isn't public.
  • Our site doesn't publish make-good terms for the 1M / 3M / 5M guarantee. FORKOFF's does.
  • A view count on its own says nothing about how the reach was built or whether it converted. Buyers should ask for more than a number.

Where we think the criticism is out of date or overstated:

  • They're not screenshots. Every launch on our homepage links to the live post on X or LinkedIn, so anyone can check the current public count, likes, reposts and replies themselves.
  • "Cinematic film" isn't what we sell. FORKOFF's comparison table calls our tier "cinematic film … distribution assumed". Our site says the opposite: "Not cinema for cinema's sake", with distribution and a view guarantee as the core offer.
  • Its description of Flowjam doesn't match Flowjam's current site either. FORKOFF's pages say Flowjam sells "the produced asset" for about "$5,000 to $10,000" with "distribution sold as a separate paid add-on". Flowjam's homepage on 8 October 2026 lists $15k and $25k packages with distribution and a written view minimum built in.

What FORKOFF's own "RADAR" audit says, and how to read it. RADAR is FORKOFF's tool for reading whether a launch's reach looks "earned or bought". It mainly compares views to likes against a roughly 500-to-1 "organic ceiling" it calibrated on 30 launches. RADAR has published readings of several launches we worked on:

Launch (LaunchVideo client) RADAR views per like RADAR label
SubQ 568 "Distribution-amplified (light)"
Enrich Labs' Helena 643 "Distribution-amplified (light)"
Mave Health 825 "Reconstructed reading", medium confidence

Read those labels the way FORKOFF itself tells you to. Its methodology page says a distribution-amplified label is "not saying" the product is fake or that anyone broke the rules. It also says "paying for distribution is legal and common, and often a sound launch tactic". Coordinated amplification is exactly the service all three of these agencies sell, FORKOFF included: its own method is a warm-up, seeding and coordinated launch-hour activity. So an "amplified" signature on a launch that hired an amplification agency is expected, not a scandal. Its own Mave Health reading says as much: "a genuine launch given a distribution lift … not a hollow buy with no conversation underneath it."

Three more caveats apply to any RADAR reading:

  1. It isn't independent. FORKOFF's methodology page states that FORKOFF "sits on both sides of the same question": it sells distribution and grades competitors' launches.
  2. The ratio is a heuristic, not a standard. FORKOFF's own page warns that a raw views-to-likes ratio can mislead on large, passive audiences. It doesn't publish a step-by-step way to reproduce a reading. Video autoplay, funding news and broad audiences all push views up faster than likes.
  3. We couldn't find a RADAR reading of a named FORKOFF client launch. The launches it showcases as "organic" (Cursor, Koji, Contra and others) are public launches by other companies, not FORKOFF's work, as its own service page says.

What actually protects a buyer is the same whichever of the three you hire: live post links, the X analytics export split into organic and promoted, a list of the accounts that amplified (and whether each was paid and disclosed), and business results you can trace. Views matter only as the first step towards those results. We cover how to check all of this in our companion guide on whether guaranteed views on X are legit, published on our blog.

The rule all three must follow: paid amplification has to be disclosed

There's a policy line every buyer should know about, whichever agency they pick. X's authenticity policy prohibits "coordinating with and/or compensating others to conduct account metric inflation" in features including likes, reposts and views. It also bans "coordinating to exchange engagement" (pods). Separately, X's paid partnerships policy allows creators to be paid to promote a product, as long as the post carries the "Paid Partnership" disclosure.

In plain English: paying real creators to post their own, disclosed take on your launch is allowed. Paying anyone to inflate the numbers is not. In the US, the FTC's 2024 rule on fake reviews and testimonials also bans buying or selling fake indicators of social media influence, "such as followers or views generated by a bot or hijacked account", where the buyer knew or should have known.

All three agencies say they use real accounts. FORKOFF says "real accounts, never a pod". Our HydraDB write-up describes using X's paid-post label. Flowjam doesn't detail its distribution method publicly. Whoever you hire, ask them to show you how paid participants are disclosed.

How to choose between them: a 7-step checklist

  1. Write down the one number that matters. Pre-orders, demos, waitlist sign-ups or investor intros, not just views. Mave Health's goal, for example, was US pre-orders, and every decision pointed at that.
  2. Work backwards from your launch date. Under two weeks rules out a 14-day warm-up. Under ten days points to a compressed launch or a rush add-on.
  3. Get the guarantee terms in writing. Ask how a view is counted (X's public view count, or impressions from analytics), the measurement window (24 hours? 7 days?), and the make-good (re-run, extra distribution, or refund).
  4. Ask for three live launch links. Open them. Look at replies and quote posts as well as the view count. Are the people engaging real buyers and operators in your market?
  5. Ask who amplifies and how they're paid. Get a sample list of accounts, their audience geography (our HydraDB launch required 33%+ US audiences), and how paid posts are disclosed.
  6. Agree the reporting. Ask for the X Post Activity export (organic vs promoted) at 48 hours, plus traffic and conversion data from your own analytics.
  7. Read the ownership and refund clauses. Flowjam states you own everything outright and that it doesn't refund. Make sure your contract with any agency says who owns the footage and cutdowns.

What it costs: putting the three on the same page

Only Flowjam lets you compare cost before a call, so here's what you can say with confidence:

  • Under $5k: none of the three publishes an offer below $5,000 for a launch video.
  • $5k: Flowjam's animated video, with no view guarantee.
  • $15k–$25k: Flowjam's filmed launch plus distribution. That's $0.15 per guaranteed view at the 100k floor ($15k ÷ 100,000), or $0.05 at the 500k floor ($25k ÷ 500,000), if the floor is exactly met.
  • Quote-based, million-view tiers: LaunchVideo (1M / 3M / 5M) and FORKOFF (1.2M / 3M / 5M). Ask each for the price per tier and divide by the guaranteed views to compare like for like.

FORKOFF's own service page says production-only quotes for a launch film "commonly land in the five to ten thousand dollar range". That's its view of the market, not a price for its own service. For a wider look at launch video budgets, see the cost guide on our blog.

The bottom line

Flowjam, The Launch Video Company and FORKOFF overlap more than their marketing suggests. All three make launch videos, all three sell guaranteed views, and all three report their own results. The real differences are practical:

  • Flowjam publishes its prices and caps its promise at 500k views.
  • LaunchVideo sells million-view tiers with production and amplification under one roof, can move fast, and quotes on a call.
  • FORKOFF sells similar tiers with the clearest public make-good, but needs a longer runway and prices only after you apply.

Whichever you shortlist, judge them on the same receipts: live posts, X's own analytics, disclosed amplifiers, and the business result you set out to move.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Flowjam, LaunchVideo and FORKOFF?+

Flowjam is a production studio with published prices ($5k / $15k / $25k) and view floors of 100k or 500k. The Launch Video Company combines founder-led production with curated X and LinkedIn amplification and guarantees 1M, 3M or 5M views, priced on a call. FORKOFF is distribution-led, guarantees 1.2M, 3M or 5M views with a published re-run-or-refund make-good, and prices by application.

Which is cheapest: Flowjam, LaunchVideo or FORKOFF?+

Flowjam is the only one with public prices, starting at $5,000 for a 60-second animated video. LaunchVideo and FORKOFF don't publish prices, so you'd need a quote from each to compare. The fairest way to compare is cost per guaranteed view at the tier you need.

Do Flowjam, LaunchVideo and FORKOFF guarantee views?+

Yes, all three do. Flowjam guarantees a 100,000-view minimum on its $15k package and 500,000 on its $25k package. LaunchVideo guarantees 1M, 3M or 5M views. FORKOFF guarantees 1.2M, 3M or 5M views.

What happens if a guaranteed view target is missed?+

FORKOFF publishes its make-good: it keeps distributing and re-runs the launch, or refunds. Flowjam and LaunchVideo don't publish make-good terms on their sites, so get them in writing before signing. Flowjam separately states that it doesn't offer refunds on its creative work.

Which agency is fastest?+

Flowjam quotes 10 days for an animated video and has a rush add-on under 7 days. LaunchVideo has run five-day launches end to end (HydraDB) and quotes 2–3 weeks for full production. FORKOFF's method needs a 14-day warm-up, and it says founders less than 21 days out are a poor fit.

Can I hire them just for distribution if I already have a video?+

LaunchVideo and FORKOFF both offer amplification only. Edison Scientific and Mave Health launched with LaunchVideo that way. Flowjam doesn't list a distribution-only tier.

Are these agencies' view counts audited?+

Not independently. All three report their own results. FORKOFF runs its own RADAR tool, but FORKOFF also sells the competing service, as its methodology page acknowledges. The best check is the live post plus X's Post Activity Dashboard export, which separates organic and promoted metrics.

Is FORKOFF's criticism of LaunchVideo fair?+

Partly. It's true that LaunchVideo's numbers are self-reported, its pricing isn't public, and its site doesn't publish make-good terms. It's not accurate that LaunchVideo relies on screenshots: every launch on its homepage links to the live post. FORKOFF also describes LaunchVideo as "cinematic film" and Flowjam as distribution-as-an-add-on, which doesn't match either company's current site.

What does "distribution-amplified" mean in FORKOFF's RADAR?+

It means views grew faster than likes compared with FORKOFF's 500-to-1 benchmark. FORKOFF says the label doesn't mean anything improper happened, and that paying for distribution is legal and common. Any launch run by an amplification agency, including FORKOFF's own, is likely to show some coordinated amplification.

Is paid amplification allowed on X?+

Paying creators to promote a product is allowed if the post uses X's Paid Partnership disclosure. Compensating people to inflate likes, reposts or views, or trading engagement in pods, breaks X's authenticity policy. In the US, the FTC also bans knowingly buying fake social media indicators such as bot views.

Which is best for a YC or AI startup launch?+

It depends on the goal. For a known budget and an animated product video, Flowjam is built around YC and AI startups. For a founder-led launch that needs millions of views on day one, LaunchVideo or FORKOFF fit better, with LaunchVideo better suited if the launch is under three weeks away.

Which is best for a LinkedIn-first B2B launch?+

LaunchVideo and FORKOFF both run organic distribution on LinkedIn as well as X. Flowjam's distribution tiers are framed around the launch post rather than a specific LinkedIn programme, so ask what's included.

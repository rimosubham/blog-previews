iGaming link building is the process of getting other websites to link to a casino, sportsbook, poker or affiliate site so that search engines trust it and rank it higher. In this niche, the links that work come from real, relevant publishers that let readers see gambling content, placed inside useful articles, with natural anchor text and clear disclosure. Volume does not win. Fit, safety and steady growth do.

That is the short answer. The rest of this guide explains why gambling link building is harder than normal SEO, which tactics still work in 2027, how to vet a site before you pay for anything, how many backlinks an iGaming site really needs, what it costs, and how to tell whether your links are paying off. It is written for operators, affiliates and anyone hiring an iGaming link building agency. If you only need the short definition, see our plain-English explainer on what iGaming link building is.

01 · Definition

What is iGaming link building?

In plain words, iGaming link building means earning or placing links on other websites that point to your gambling site. "iGaming" covers online casinos, sports betting, poker, bingo, lottery, fantasy sports and crypto casinos. A backlink is simply a clickable link on someone else's page that sends readers (and search engines) to yours.

Google has said for years that links help it find pages and understand how relevant they are. Its own link best practices explain how Google reads links and anchor text. So links still matter. What has changed is how strict Google is about links that were made only to push rankings.

iGaming link building is the same idea as link building in any other industry. The difference is the risk. Gambling is a "your money or your life" type of topic, it is regulated country by country, and it attracts more spam than almost any other niche. That means every link gets more scrutiny, from Google and from publishers.

02 · The hard truth

Why is gambling link building harder than normal SEO?

If you have built links for a SaaS or e-commerce site, iGaming will feel different. Here is why.

1. Most publishers say no. Many mainstream sites have a blanket policy against gambling content. Others accept it only for certain countries or only with a "sponsored" label. Your pool of possible sites is much smaller.

Heads up

Gambling is a YMYL-style topic with country-by-country rules. A link that looks fine for rankings can still be a brand or licensing risk.

2. Prices are higher. Because fewer sites accept gambling links, the ones that do charge more. It is common for the same site to quote a much higher price for a casino link than for a normal link.

3. Ads are restricted, so organic search matters more. Paid ads for gambling are limited. Google, for example, only allows gambling ads in certain countries and only for certified advertisers under its gambling and games ad policy. That pushes more operators into SEO, which makes rankings even more competitive.

4. There is a lot of spam. Private blog networks (PBNs), hacked pages, expired domains and "casino link packages" are everywhere. Google's spam policies name buying or selling links that pass ranking credit, excessive link exchanges and automated link schemes as link spam. In a niche full of this, clean links stand out, and dirty ones get you caught.

5. Regulation and compliance. Each country has its own gambling rules. In Great Britain, for example, the Gambling Commission licenses operators and the ASA's CAP Code has a full section on gambling marketing. A link on a page that targets minors or a market where you are not licensed is a brand risk, not just an SEO risk.

6. Geo-targeting. A UK casino needs links from UK-relevant pages. A Brazilian sportsbook needs Portuguese, Brazil-focused pages. Translating one campaign into ten languages rarely works.

Put simply: you have fewer options, higher costs and less room for error. That is why a plan matters more here than anywhere else.

03 · Quality checks

What makes a good iGaming backlink?

Domain Rating (DR) or Domain Authority alone does not make a link good. Plenty of high-DR sites sell links to anyone and have almost no real readers. Use these checks instead. Our full method is in how we vet iGaming publishers.

1. Topical relevance

The linking page should make sense for your site. Gambling, sports, esports, gaming, finance, fintech, crypto, entertainment and local news sites are usually a good fit. A casino link inside a gardening blog is not.

2. Real organic traffic

Check that the site, and ideally the section your link sits in, gets real search traffic from the country you target. A site with high DR but falling or tiny traffic is a warning sign.

3. Editorial standards

Read a few recent posts. Do they have real authors? Is the writing edited? Would the site ever reject a submission? A publisher that publishes anything is a publisher Google may stop trusting.

4. Clean outbound links

Look at who else the site links to. If every post links to casinos, loans, CBD and essay services with exact-match anchors, the site is a link farm. Your link will sit in bad company.

5. A contextual, in-content placement

The best link sits in the body of an article, next to text that explains why the reader should click. Footer, sidebar and "partners" page links carry little weight and look paid.

6. Indexing and permanence

Make sure the page you get is indexed in Google and stays live. Ask how long the placement lasts and what happens if it is removed.

04 · Nine tactics

How do you build backlinks for a casino site? 9 tactics that work

Here are the tactics that still work for casino and sportsbook link building, roughly from lowest to highest risk.

1. Original data and research

Publishers love numbers they can quote. If you run a casino or affiliate site, you may have anonymised data on popular games, payment methods, support questions or betting trends around big sports events. A short, honest study can earn links from news, sports and finance sites that would never link to a review page.

Keep it simple: one clear question, a clear method, and a few charts. Then pitch it to journalists who cover that topic.

2. Digital PR and expert commentary

Digital PR means pitching stories, not links. Examples: a comment on a new gambling law, a responsible gambling survey, or an analysis of odds before a big final. Trade media such as gambling news sites, sports business outlets and regional news are good targets.

You can also join journalist request services and answer questions as an expert. This builds high-trust links and brand mentions, though it takes time.

3. Guest posts on relevant publishers

A guest post is an article you write for another site, with a link back to yours. It still works when the article is genuinely useful for that site's readers, for example a guide to bankroll management on a sports site, or a payments explainer on a fintech blog. It fails when it is a thin article written only to hold a link.

4. Niche edits (link insertions)

Not sure which to choose? Read our comparison of guest posts vs link insertions.

A niche edit is a link added to an existing article that already ranks and has traffic. Because the page is already indexed and trusted, results can show faster than with a new post. The risk is the vendor: many "niche edit" sellers place links on spun content or hacked pages. Vet every page with the checklist above before you pay.

5. Linkable tools and resources

A useful free tool earns links again and again. Ideas for iGaming:

  • An odds converter or bet calculator
  • A wagering requirement calculator for bonuses
  • A house edge or RTP comparison table
  • A country-by-country guide to gambling laws

Tools also give you a natural "bridge": publishers link to the tool, and the tool links internally to your money pages.

6. Broken link building

Find dead links on relevant sites (for example, a broken link to a closed gambling statistics page), create a better resource on the same topic, and suggest it as a replacement. Tools like Ahrefs or Semrush make this faster. It is slow but very clean.

7. Unlinked brand mentions

Search for places that already mention your brand without a link, then politely ask the writer to add one. This is one of the easiest, safest wins for established operators.

8. Partner, supplier and affiliate-program links

Game providers, payment partners, sponsorship deals, events and industry associations often have partner pages or case studies. These links are relevant by nature. Just remember that paid sponsorship links should be marked as sponsored.

9. Local-market links

For each country you target, build links from that country's sites, in that language. Local sports blogs, local news, local finance sites and local affiliate sites send stronger signals than a translated global campaign.

05 · Red flags

What to avoid: tactics that get casino sites penalised

Some tactics still "work" for a few months and then wipe out a site. Avoid:

  • Private blog networks (PBNs). Networks of sites built only to pass links. Google is good at spotting them. We explain why we refuse PBN casino backlinks.
  • Hacked or injected links. Links planted on pages without the owner's consent. This is both a spam and an ethics problem.
  • Site-wide footer and sidebar links. Hundreds of identical links from one domain look paid.
  • Cheap bulk packages. "100 casino links for $99" means low-quality sites, spun content or both.
  • Exact-match anchor overload. Using "best online casino" again and again as anchor text is one of the fastest routes to a filter.
  • Parasite pages and site reputation abuse. Renting a section on a big site to publish casino content that has nothing to do with that site. Google added a specific policy against this in its March 2024 spam update.
  • Links to the wrong markets. Links from sites that target countries where you are not licensed, or audiences that include minors.

If Google decides your links are unnatural, you may see a manual action in Search Console. Google explains how these work in its Manual Actions report help page. Fixing one takes months, so prevention is much cheaper.

06 · Buying links

Is buying iGaming backlinks safe?

This is the question everyone asks and few answer honestly.

The truth: Google's rules say links that are paid for should not pass ranking credit, and asks you to mark them with rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow", as explained in its guide to qualifying outbound links. At the same time, almost every gambling site that ranks has paid for placements in some form, through content fees, guest post fees or agency retainers.

So "safe" is about risk, not rules alone. Buying links is riskier when:

  • The site sells links to anyone, on any topic.
  • The content is thin and exists only to hold your link.
  • You buy many links fast, with the same exact-match anchors.
  • The vendor will not show you the site before you pay.

It is much lower risk when the publisher is real, the content is useful to its readers, the link sits naturally in context, the placement is disclosed where required, and growth is steady. That is why working from a vetted publisher inventory, where you can see the actual site, its traffic and its rules before buying, is safer than buying blind packages. Advertising rules can also apply: in the UK, the CAP Code requires marketing to be obviously identifiable as marketing.

Our honest view: treat any paid placement as advertising and judge it on whether it would make sense even if it passed no SEO value. If yes, it is probably a good placement. If no, skip it.

07 · Anchors

Anchor text for iGaming links

Anchor text is the clickable text of a link. In gambling SEO it is one of the most watched signals. The guide from Moz on anchor text covers the basics; here is how it applies to iGaming.

A safe anchor profile usually looks like this:

  • Branded anchors ("iGamingPublishers", "YourCasino") should be the biggest group.
  • URL anchors ("yourcasino.com") are natural and safe.
  • Topical or partial-match anchors ("this guide to casino bonuses") add relevance.
  • Generic anchors ("here", "this site") appear naturally in real writing.
  • Exact-match anchors ("best online casino") should be rare and spread out over time.

There is no magic ratio that works for every site. Look at the sites that rank for your target keyword and stay close to what is normal in your market. Most importantly, match the anchor to the page: branded anchors to the home page, descriptive anchors to guides, and only occasional keyword anchors to money pages. We cover this in depth in our guide to anchor text for iGaming links.

08 · Volume

How many backlinks does an iGaming site need?

There is no single number. It depends on three things (our full benchmark is in how many backlinks a casino site needs):

  • 1. The keyword. A long-tail page such as "casino bonus wagering calculator" may rank with a handful of good links. "Best online casino UK" may need hundreds of strong referring domains.
  • 2. The market. Some markets are far more competitive than others. The UK and US are hard. Smaller or newer markets can be much easier.
  • 3. Your competitors. This is the best guide you have.

Here is a simple way to work out your own target:

  • 1. Search your target keyword in the target country.
  • 2. Take the top 5 ranking pages.
  • 3. Check how many referring domains (unique sites, not total links) point to each page and to each domain, using a tool like Ahrefs or Semrush.
  • 4. Look at the quality of those links, not only the count.
  • 5. Plan to match the average over time, with better-quality links.

Also remember that links to the domain as a whole help every page, while links to a specific page help that page most. Strong internal linking spreads authority from pages that earn links (guides, tools, data) to pages that make money (reviews, bonus pages).

A useful rule: for a new site, focus first on 10 to 30 high-quality, relevant referring domains to the home page and best guides, then build page-level links to your key money pages. Growing steadily every month looks far more natural than a sudden spike.

11 · 90-day plan

A simple 90-day iGaming link building plan

Days 1 to 15: set the base

  • Audit your current backlinks and anchor text. Disavow only if you have a clear toxic-link problem.
  • Fix technical SEO and internal linking so link value can flow.
  • Pick 5 to 10 target pages and the keywords for each.
  • Study the top-ranking competitors for those keywords.

Days 16 to 45: build linkable assets

  • Improve your target pages so they deserve links (clear answers, original data, honest reviews, responsible gambling info).
  • Create one linkable asset: a small data study, a tool or a definitive guide.
  • Build a list of relevant, vetted publishers in each market.

Days 46 to 90: run a controlled campaign

  • Start outreach and placements at a steady pace, not all at once.
  • Mix tactics: guest posts, a few niche edits, PR pitches and partner links.
  • Keep anchors mostly branded and natural.
  • Track every link: URL, anchor, date, target page, indexing status.

12 · Measurement

How to measure iGaming link building results

Do not judge success by link count or DR. Track these instead:

  • Rankings of the target pages for their keywords, by country.
  • Organic traffic to those pages.
  • Conversions: registrations, first-time deposits (FTDs) for operators, or clicks to operators for affiliates.
  • Referring domains gained and lost each month.
  • Link health: are the links still live, indexed and unchanged? Our guide to link decay in iGaming explains how to keep them live.
  • Referral traffic from the links themselves (a sign the publisher has real readers).

Links can take weeks or months to show impact. Look at trends over 3 to 6 months, not day by day. If your DR is rising but rankings and FTDs are flat, your links are probably on the wrong sites or pointing at the wrong pages.

13 · Compliance

Responsible gambling and compliance in link building

Compliance is not just a legal box to tick. It also makes your links safer:

  • Only build links in markets where you (or the brands you promote) are licensed.
  • Avoid sites and content aimed at under-18s.
  • Make sure the content around your link does not promise easy wins or "risk-free" betting.
  • Include responsible gambling messages and links to help services, such as BeGambleAware in the UK, on your own pages.
  • Label sponsored content where the law or platform requires it.

Publishers with good editorial standards will often ask for this anyway. Treat it as a sign you are working with the right sites.

14 · Mistakes

Common iGaming link building mistakes

  • Buying links before your pages are good enough to rank.
  • Pointing every link at one money page with the same anchor.
  • Chasing DR instead of traffic and relevance.
  • Ignoring local markets and using one global list of sites.
  • Not tracking links, so removed or deindexed links go unnoticed.
  • Stopping and starting campaigns, which creates unnatural spikes.

Conclusion

iGaming link building works when you treat it like building a real reputation, not like buying a ranking. Get links from relevant, real publishers in the markets you serve. Place them in useful content with natural anchor text. Grow steadily, track everything and stay compliant. Avoid PBNs, bulk packages and exact-match spam, no matter how cheap.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is iGaming link building?

iGaming link building is getting other websites to link to an online casino, sportsbook, poker or gambling affiliate site. These backlinks help search engines trust the site and rank it higher. In this niche, the best links come from relevant, real publishers, placed in useful content with natural anchor text.

How do you build backlinks for a casino site?

Use original data and research, digital PR, guest posts on relevant sites, carefully vetted niche edits, useful tools such as odds or bonus calculators, broken link building, unlinked brand mentions and partner links. Vet every site for relevance, real traffic and clean outbound links before you get a link from it.

Why is gambling link building harder than normal SEO?

Fewer publishers accept gambling content, prices are higher, paid ads are restricted, the niche is full of spam, and gambling is regulated in each country. That means fewer safe options and less room for mistakes than in most industries.

How many backlinks does an iGaming site need?

It depends on the keyword, the market and your competitors. Check how many referring domains the top five ranking pages have, then aim to match that over time with better-quality links. Easy long-tail pages may need only a few links, while top casino keywords can need hundreds of strong referring domains.

Is buying iGaming backlinks safe?

Google's rules say paid links should be marked as sponsored or nofollow. In practice, risk depends on quality. Blind bulk packages, PBNs and exact-match anchors are high risk. Disclosed placements on real, relevant publishers with useful content and steady growth are much lower risk.

What is an iGaming link building agency?

It is a company that plans and places backlinks for gambling sites, usually through outreach, guest posts, niche edits and digital PR. A good agency lets you approve sites first, checks traffic at page level, does not use PBNs and reports every live link.

How long does iGaming link building take to work?

Most sites see movement in 2 to 6 months, depending on competition, the quality of links and the state of the target pages. Very competitive keywords in markets like the UK and US can take longer.

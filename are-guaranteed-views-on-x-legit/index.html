Are Guaranteed Views on X Legit? Questions to Ask Before You Sign

How X counts a view, what a launch agency can honestly promise, and how to check a view guarantee before, during and after launch day.

Subah Wadhwani · Co-founder & CEO at Atomik Growth, the company behind The Launch Video CompanyUpdated 8 Oct 2026 · 18 min read · Checked against 11 sources
Featured image: Are guaranteed views on X legit? A verification checklist (placeholder)

In short: Guaranteed views on X can be legit, but only some kinds. Views bought from view-selling sites are typically bots. They break X's authenticity rules, and the US FTC bans knowingly buying fake views. Paid X ads are legitimate and show up as "promoted" in X analytics. Agency guarantees, such as LaunchVideo's 1M / 3M / 5M tiers or the 100k-to-5M tiers other launch agencies sell, are legit when real, relevant accounts share the post and paid posts are disclosed. X counts a view every time a logged-in user sees a post, repeats included, so a view isn't a unique person. Before you sign, get the counting method, the measurement window, the make-good, analytics access and the list of amplifiers in writing.

Disclosure: this guide is published by The Launch Video Company, which sells guaranteed-view launches on X and LinkedIn. That's exactly why we think buyers should know how to check us and everyone else. Platform rules are quoted from X's own help pages and the FTC as of 8 October 2026.

The short answer: legit, scam, or somewhere in between?

When people ask whether guaranteed views on X are legit, they usually mean one of three very different things:

  1. A website that sells "100,000 X views for $49". These are almost never legit. Even the AI-generated answers in search results for this question warn that such services are "generated by automated bot networks, click farms, or inactive scripts". X's rules prohibit compensated "metric inflation" of views. You'd get a bigger number and nothing else.
  2. Paid X ads. Completely legit. You pay X to show your post as an ad, and the extra views appear in your analytics as promoted, separately from organic views.
  3. A launch agency guaranteeing a view tier. This can be legit, depending on how the views are delivered. Real founders, operators, investors and creators who quote and repost your launch, with paid posts disclosed, is a legitimate service. Buying bot traffic to hit a number is not, whoever is selling it.

Most of this guide is about the third kind, because that's what startups are actually being sold for launch day, and it's where the useful questions are.

How X actually counts a view

Start with X's own definition, because a lot of confusion comes from assuming "views" means "people".

According to X's View counts FAQ:

  • Anyone logged in who views a post counts as a view, wherever they see it (Home, Search, Profiles and so on) and whether or not they follow the author.
  • If you're the author, looking at your own post also counts.
  • Views aren't unique. "You could look at a post on web and then on your phone, and that would count as two views."
  • Promoted posts show view counts too.
  • Some posts have no view count at all, including Community posts, Circle posts and older posts. A count can take up to a minute to appear.

So a 1M-view post hasn't been seen by a million different people. It has been shown about a million times, to logged-in users, including repeat views.

Views vs impressions vs video views

X reports several "seen it" numbers, and agencies don't always say which one they mean:

Metric Where you see it What it measures (per X) What it doesn't tell you
Views Public count on every post Logged-in views of the post, repeats included Unique people, attention, intent
Impressions Post analytics / Post Activity Dashboard Times the post was served on X's surfaces (X's glossary wording); organic and promoted reported in separate rows Whether anyone stopped scrolling
Video views / view rate Video Activity Dashboard Video watched with 50% in view for 2+ seconds, or expanded/unmuted Whether they watched past the hook
Engagements Post analytics Clicks anywhere on the post: reposts, replies, follows, likes, links, media, profile, expand Whether engagers are real buyers
Retention / completion rate Video Activity Dashboard Average % watched; % who finished Whether viewers converted

All of these come from X's Post and Video Activity Dashboards help page. It also says the dashboards are free (with an ads account or by registering at analytics.x.com), keep 28 days of data by default, and export up to 30 days at a time. Metrics "take up to 36 hours to stabilize".

What this means for a guarantee: a contract should name the metric. "1M views" usually means the public view count on the launch post. That's the easiest number to check and the easiest to misunderstand. If an agency counts views across several posts (quote posts, reposts of a clip, creator posts), it should say so.

Here's the part many buyers miss: "organic" in agency language usually means "not an X ad", not "uncoordinated" or "unpaid". When we say we drive organic views, we mean the views come from real people seeing real posts in their feeds, not from X's ad system. That reach is coordinated. Every launch agency is, by definition, coordinating attention. The question is whether it's done with real, relevant, disclosed accounts.

Where X draws the line on paid amplification

Three rules decide whether a guaranteed-views campaign is above board.

1. X's authenticity policy. X's authenticity policy says you may not "artificially impact how content is discovered and amplified". Under "Engagement Spam" it specifically prohibits:

  • "coordinating to exchange engagement in any X features, such as Likes, Polls, Replies, Reposts, Lists, Views, or Follows" (engagement pods);
  • "coordinating with and/or compensating others to conduct account metric inflation" in those same features;
  • "using or promoting third-party services" to do any of the above.

It also explicitly allows "authentically coordinating with others to express ideas, viewpoints, support, or opposition towards a cause in a non-violative manner." Enforcement can include restricting reach, removing posts from For You and Following timelines, and suspension.

2. X's paid partnerships policy. Under X's paid partnerships policy, creators can be paid, gifted products or given commission to promote a product. But when that content goes out as an organic post, it "must have the 'Paid Partnership' disclosure on" (a toggle in Content Disclosure settings). Some categories, such as supplements, financial products in some regions and gambling, can't be promoted this way at all.

3. US consumer protection law. The FTC's 2024 final rule on fake reviews and testimonials "prohibits anyone from selling or buying fake indicators of social media influence, such as followers or views generated by a bot or hijacked account", where the buyer knew or should have known they were fake and used them commercially. Separately, the FTC's Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers says anyone with a "material connection" to a brand (paid, gifted, employed, family) must disclose it clearly. It adds that "tags, likes, pins, and similar ways of showing you like a brand or product can be endorsements."

Put simply: paying real people to share their honest, disclosed take on your launch is fine. Paying anyone to push the numbers up, or joining a pod that trades likes and reposts, is not. If an agency won't tell you which of those it's doing, that's your answer.

Paid amplification vs organic reach: what each looks like in the data

You can't see an agency's invoices, but you can see the post. Here's what different kinds of reach usually look like:

Genuinely viral, uncoordinated posts tend to collect engagement in a long, irregular tail as new people find them over hours and days. Likes, replies and reposts rise roughly with views.

A well-run coordinated launch usually shows a strong first hour. That's when the founder's network, investors and briefed creators quote and reply, which is exactly what the algorithm rewards early. Engagement is real but front-loaded, and the replies come from recognisable people in your industry. Our launch-mechanics breakdown explains why that first window matters.

Bought or botted reach shows views running far ahead of everything else: lots of views, few likes, almost no replies, and replies that are generic ("Great project!"), off-topic or in languages your buyers don't speak.

Paid ads show up plainly in X's Post Activity Dashboard. Each promoted post gets an organic row and a separate "Promoted in X campaign(s)" row.

The views-per-like ratio (and its limits)

One public heuristic is the views-per-like ratio: divide a post's views by its likes. Third-party view-audit tools are built on it. The best-known one we've seen is run by an audit-led launch agency that competes with us. Its methodology treats roughly 500 views per like as an "organic ceiling", calibrated on 30 launches, and its free checker treats roughly 5,000 views per like as a "fraud-tier" signature. In its own audit of 30 public launches, 10 read organic and 20 showed a distribution-amplified signature.

It's a useful smell test, and you can run it yourself on any post in seconds. But keep its limits in mind:

  • It's an agency's heuristic, not an X metric. X publishes no "normal" ratio. The tool's own methodology states that its owner "sits on both sides of the same question", because it sells distribution and grades launches.
  • It flags coordination, not fraud. The same methodology says an "amplified" label doesn't mean anyone broke the rules, and that "paying for distribution is legal and common". Any agency-run launch, ours and the tool owner's included, is coordinated by design.
  • It can mislead. The methodology itself notes that a raw ratio misleads on large, passive audiences. Autoplay video and broad news (like a funding round) can add views without adding likes.

Here's how the ratio looks on a few public posts that tool has read. The first is a large AI lab's own launch; the other two are launches we worked on:

Public launch post Views Likes Views per like Audit tool's reading
A large AI lab's chip launch 7.1M 22.7K 312 Organic
AI model launch (LaunchVideo) 13.1M 23.1K 568 "Distribution-amplified (light)"

Use the ratio as one signal among several, never as a verdict on its own.

How to verify a view guarantee: a step-by-step process

Before you sign

  1. Name the metric. Is it the public view count on the launch post, total impressions across all posts, or video views? Get it in writing.
  2. Fix the window. Is it 24 hours, 72 hours or 7 days? X's metrics take up to 36 hours to settle, so a 48–72 hour window is a fair minimum.
  3. Agree the make-good. What happens if the tier is missed: more distribution, a re-run, a partial refund or a full refund? In our comparison of launch video agency models (on our blog), only the audit-led agency we reviewed publishes its make-good on its site ("re-run the play … or we refund"). At least one fixed-price package studio says it doesn't offer refunds on creative work. Whoever you hire, put it in the contract.
  4. Ask how the views are delivered. Creators? Founder and investor networks? Paid X ads? Clipping accounts? Each is legitimate if disclosed, but you should know the mix.
  5. Ask for a sample amplifier list. Real names, handles and audience geography. Our brain-headset launch write-up describes tiering creators, and on another launch we checked every influencer for a 33%+ US audience. Ask any agency for the equivalent.
  6. Ask how paid posts are disclosed. Paid creators should use X's Paid Partnership label. One of our five-day launches used X's paid-post label "to stay within X's guidelines."
  7. Ask for three past launches you can click. Live posts, not screenshots. Check the replies.

On launch day

  1. Watch the first 90 minutes. Are the first quotes and replies from people you recognise in your industry, or from accounts with no history?
  2. Spot-check amplifier accounts. Open a few profiles. Do they post about your space? Do their followers look like real people?
  3. Check the reply section. Real launches get questions, objections and jokes. Bot reach gets rows of fire emojis and copy-paste praise.

After launch (48–72 hours)

  1. Pull the Post Activity export from analytics.x.com (Posts tab) or ads.x.com → Analytics → Post activity. Check the organic vs promoted rows. If the agency claimed organic delivery, the promoted row should be empty or match ads you knowingly ran.
  2. Check video metrics in the Video Activity Dashboard: view rate, retention and completion. Bot views rarely produce healthy retention.
  3. Compute engagement coherence. Views per like, plus replies, reposts, quotes and bookmarks relative to views. Compare against your own previous posts.
  4. Trace outcomes. UTM-tagged links, signups, demo requests, pre-orders, investor emails. This is the number that should have been in the brief all along.

Questions to ask before you sign a guaranteed-views contract

Copy this list into your next agency call:

  • ☐ Which metric is guaranteed: post views, impressions or video views? On which post(s)?
  • ☐ Over what window, measured when, and from which source (public count or X analytics)?
  • ☐ What exactly happens if the guarantee is missed? Is that in the contract?
  • ☐ Where do the views come from: creators, networks, ads or clipping? What's the rough mix?
  • ☐ Can I see a sample list of amplifiers, with audience geography?
  • ☐ Are any participants paid? How will their posts be disclosed?
  • ☐ Do you use engagement pods, purchased views or automation? (The only acceptable answer is no.)
  • ☐ Will you share the Post Activity export or a screen-share at 48–72 hours?
  • ☐ Can I see three past launches as live links, plus what they led to (signups, demos, revenue)?
  • ☐ Who writes the copy for amplifiers, and do I approve it?
  • ☐ What do you need from me (founder network, launch date, assets), and by when?
  • ☐ What are you not able to promise for a launch like mine?

Do guaranteed views turn into signups, demos or revenue?

Sometimes, and only when the views reach the right people. A million views from the wrong country or the wrong industry is worth less than 100,000 from your buyers. That's why the most useful clause in any guarantee isn't the number but the audience.

Some self-reported examples, from agencies' own sites:

  • AI marketing-agent launch (LaunchVideo): 3.6M views, 10× homepage traffic, 9,000+ sign-ups and customers. The full story is in How we got 3.6M views & 9,000 demos on a Monday morning launch.
  • Brain-headset launch (LaunchVideo, amplification only): 2.5M+ views and six figures of pre-orders for a $500 headset, with 80% of the audience from the US.
  • A fixed-price package studio credits one founder-led film with landing a major enterprise customer.

None of these are independently audited, ours included. Ask for the attribution data behind any outcome claim, and set up your own tracking (UTMs, a launch-specific landing page, a "how did you hear about us" field) before launch day. The bottom line

Guaranteed views on X are only as legit as the way they're delivered. Bought views from view-selling sites are bots: against X's rules, illegal to knowingly buy in the US, and useless for a launch. Paid X ads are legitimate and clearly labelled as promoted in your analytics. Agency guarantees can be legitimate too, when real, relevant people share your launch and paid posts are disclosed. But a number is only a promise until you can check it.

So check it. Name the metric, the window and the make-good. Ask who the amplifiers are and how they're disclosed. Pull X's own analytics 48–72 hours later. Above all, judge the launch on what it moved: signups, demos, pre-orders, investor intros.

Frequently asked questions

Are guaranteed views on X legit?+

It depends on how they're delivered. Views bought from view-selling websites are usually bots, break X's authenticity policy, and are banned under the FTC's 2024 rule when knowingly bought. Paid X ads and agency launches that use real, disclosed creators and networks are legitimate. Always verify with X's own analytics.

How does X count views?+

According to X's help center, any logged-in user who views a post counts as a view, wherever they see it and whether or not they follow the author. Views aren't unique: viewing on web and then on phone counts twice, and authors' own views count too.

Are X views the same as impressions?+

They're closely related, but X reports them in different places. Views are the public count on every post. Impressions appear in post analytics and the Post Activity Dashboard, where organic and promoted impressions are split into separate rows. Neither is a count of unique people.

Is it against X's rules to pay for views?+

Paying people or services to inflate views, likes, reposts or follows breaks X's authenticity policy, and so does trading engagement in pods. Paying for X ads is allowed. Paying creators to post about your product is allowed if they use X's Paid Partnership disclosure.

Can buying views get my account penalised?+

Yes. X's authenticity policy lists enforcement such as restricting reach, removing posts from For You and Following timelines, locking accounts and suspension for engagement spam, including using third-party services to inflate metrics.

How can I tell if a post's views are fake?+

Look at engagement relative to views (likes, replies, reposts, bookmarks), the quality and language of replies, whether amplifier accounts are real people in your industry, the audience's geography, and video retention in X's Video Activity Dashboard. Views running thousands of times ahead of likes is a strong warning sign.

What is a normal views-per-like ratio on X?+

X doesn't publish one. One third-party view-audit tool, run by a launch agency, uses about 500 views per like as an "organic ceiling" and about 5,000 as a "fraud-tier" line, based on its own sample of launches. Treat these as rough heuristics, not standards.

What does "organic views" mean when an agency says it?+

Usually that the views didn't come from X ads, so they appear in the organic rows of X analytics. It doesn't mean the launch wasn't coordinated or that nobody was paid. Ask how the views are delivered and how paid participants are disclosed.

What should a guaranteed-views contract include?+

The metric (post views, impressions or video views), the posts it applies to, the measurement window, the data source, the make-good if the tier is missed, analytics access, a sample amplifier list, and a commitment to disclosure and no bots, pods or purchased views.

How does LaunchVideo's view guarantee work?+

LaunchVideo's site offers guaranteed 1M, 3M or 5M view tiers, delivered through founder-led video production and a curated network of tech, founder, operator and investor accounts on X and LinkedIn, or as amplification only. Pricing and the guarantee terms are scoped on an intro call. Ask for the window and make-good in writing.

Do other agencies guarantee views on X?+

Yes. Audit-led launch agencies sell similar tiers (about 1.2M, 3M or 5M views), and at least one publishes a re-run-or-refund make-good. Fixed-price package studios typically guarantee 100,000 or 500,000-view minimums on $15k and $25k packages.

Do guaranteed views lead to signups?+

Only if the views reach your buyers. Agencies report examples such as 9,000+ sign-ups after a 3.6M-view launch, but these figures are self-reported. Set up your own attribution before launch and judge the result on conversions, not views.
